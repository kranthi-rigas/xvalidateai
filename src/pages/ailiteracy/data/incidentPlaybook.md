## 1. Detection

AI incidents should be reported by staff, students, or parents through the organisation's standard reporting channels. An AI incident includes any event where an AI system produces harmful, biased, inaccurate, or unexpected outputs that affect individuals or operations.

### Examples of AI Incidents
- AI tool generates inappropriate or biased content
- Student data is exposed through an AI platform
- AI-based grading produces unfair or inaccurate results
- AI chatbot provides harmful or misleading advice

---

## 2. Assessment

Once an incident is reported, the designated AI lead or compliance officer should assess the severity using the following scale:

- **Low** — Minor inaccuracy with no direct harm; easily corrected.
- **Medium** — Noticeable impact on a small group; requires review and remediation.
- **High** — Significant harm or data breach affecting multiple individuals.
- **Critical** — Widespread harm, legal implications, or regulatory breach.

---

## 3. Containment

Take immediate action to limit further impact:

- **Stop** further use of the AI tool if it poses ongoing risk
- **Restrict** access to the tool pending investigation
- **Preserve** evidence including screenshots, logs, and outputs
- **Isolate** affected data or systems where necessary

---

## 4. Notification

Inform the appropriate stakeholders based on severity:

- **Low**: Line manager and IT team
- **Medium**: Senior leadership and data protection officer
- **High**: Governing body, legal counsel, and affected individuals
- **Critical**: Regulatory authorities (e.g., ICO), governing body, and all affected parties

Notifications should be made within **72 hours** for incidents involving personal data breaches, in accordance with GDPR requirements.

---

## 5. Investigation

Conduct a thorough investigation to understand the root cause:

1. **Gather evidence** — Collect all relevant logs, outputs, and user reports
2. **Interview witnesses** — Speak with staff and users involved in the incident
3. **Analyse the AI system** — Determine whether the issue stems from training data, model configuration, or misuse
4. **Identify contributing factors** — Consider human error, lack of training, or inadequate policies
5. **Document findings** — Record all evidence and analysis in the incident log

---

## 6. Remediation

Based on investigation findings, take corrective action:

- Update AI usage policies and guidelines
- Provide additional training to affected staff
- Reconfigure or replace the AI tool if necessary
- Implement additional safeguards or monitoring
- Communicate changes to all relevant stakeholders

---

## 7. Documentation

Maintain a complete record of the incident:

- Date and time of detection
- Description of the incident
- Severity classification
- Actions taken for containment
- Notification log
- Investigation findings
- Remediation steps implemented
- Lessons learned

All records should be stored securely and retained in accordance with the organisation's data retention policy.

---

## 8. Review and Improvement

After resolving the incident:

- Conduct a **post-incident review** with all involved parties
- Update the **AI risk register** to reflect new risks identified
- Revise **AI policies** and **training materials** as needed
- Share anonymised **lessons learned** across the organisation
- Schedule a **follow-up review** to ensure remediation effectiveness

---

> **Note:** This playbook should be reviewed and updated at least annually, or following any significant AI incident.
